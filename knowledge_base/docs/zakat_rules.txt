ZAKAT RULES AND REGULATIONS IN ISLAM
=====================================

DEFINITION OF ZAKAT
Zakat is one of the Five Pillars of Islam. It is an obligatory annual payment made by eligible Muslims on their accumulated wealth above a minimum threshold called Nisab. The word Zakat means purification and growth in Arabic. By paying Zakat, a Muslim purifies their remaining wealth and fulfills a religious duty to the poor and needy.

NISAB THRESHOLD
Nisab is the minimum amount of wealth a Muslim must possess before Zakat becomes obligatory. There are two standards for calculating Nisab:

Gold Standard Nisab: equivalent to 7.5 tola or 87.48 grams of gold. This is the higher threshold and means fewer people are obligated to pay.

Silver Standard Nisab: equivalent to 52.5 tola or 612.36 grams of silver. This is the lower threshold and is considered more conservative. Many scholars prefer the silver standard because it includes more people in the obligation and therefore benefits more poor people.

In Pakistan, the silver standard Nisab is commonly used as it is more inclusive.

HAWL - THE LUNAR YEAR CONDITION
Hawl means that a Muslim must have owned wealth above the Nisab threshold for one complete Islamic lunar year before Zakat becomes due. If wealth falls below Nisab at any point during the year, the Hawl resets. Newly acquired wealth during the year is added to the calculation at the end of the year, not from the date it was acquired.

ZAKAT RATE
The Zakat rate is 2.5 percent of total zakatable wealth. This rate is fixed and agreed upon by all four major Sunni schools of Islamic law: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali.

ZAKATABLE ASSETS
The following assets are subject to Zakat:

Cash and bank balances: All cash held at home or in bank accounts, including savings accounts, current accounts, and fixed deposits. The full balance is zakatable.

Gold and silver: The market value of all gold and silver owned, regardless of whether it is jewelry, coins, bars, or any other form. However, scholars differ on personal use jewelry - see the jewelry section below.

Business inventory: The market value of goods held for sale in a business. This does not include business equipment, machinery, or property used in running the business.

Receivables and loans given: Money owed to you by others that you expect to receive. If you have lent money to someone and they are able to repay, that amount is zakatable. If the loan is unlikely to be recovered, scholars differ on whether Zakat is due.

Investments and shares: The zakatable portion of shares in companies is calculated based on the company's zakatable assets per share. For listed companies, many scholars use a simplified approach of applying 25 percent to 30 percent of the share price as a proxy for the zakatable portion. Islamic mutual funds provide Zakat calculation statements to their investors.

Agricultural produce: Zakat on agricultural produce is called Ushr and has different rules and rates. It is due at harvest time, not annually.

GOLD JEWELRY - SCHOLARLY DIFFERENCE
There is a well-known scholarly difference of opinion regarding gold jewelry used personally by women:

Opinion 1 (Hanafi school): Gold jewelry is always zakatable regardless of whether it is worn regularly for personal use. This is the stricter opinion.

Opinion 2 (Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools): Gold jewelry that is regularly worn for personal use is exempt from Zakat. Only jewelry kept as savings or investment is zakatable.

In Pakistan, many scholars follow the Hanafi opinion that jewelry is zakatable. Users should consult their local scholar for a personal ruling.

NON-ZAKATABLE ASSETS
The following assets are NOT subject to Zakat:
- Primary residence (the home you live in)
- Personal vehicles used for daily transport
- Household furniture and appliances
- Business equipment, machinery, and tools
- Land and property held for personal use (not for sale or investment)
- Clothing and personal items

DEDUCTIONS ALLOWED
The following debts and liabilities can be deducted from zakatable wealth:
- Short-term debts due within the current year
- Unpaid bills and immediate expenses due
- The current year's installment of a long-term loan (not the full loan amount)
- Taxes due to the government

RECIPIENTS OF ZAKAT - EIGHT CATEGORIES
The Quran in Surah At-Tawbah (9:60) specifies eight categories of people eligible to receive Zakat:
1. Al-Fuqara: The poor who have nothing or very little
2. Al-Masakeen: The needy who have some income but not enough
3. Al-Amileen: Workers and administrators of Zakat collection
4. Al-Muallafat Qulubuhum: Those whose hearts are to be reconciled
5. Ar-Riqab: For freeing slaves (historically applicable)
6. Al-Gharimeen: Those in debt who cannot repay
7. Fi Sabilillah: In the way of Allah, for Islamic causes and education
8. Ibn As-Sabil: Travelers who are stranded and in need

ZAKAT ON PROPERTY FOR SALE
If a person owns property (land, apartment, commercial space) with the intention of selling it, that property is zakatable based on its current market value. Property held for personal use or as a long-term investment for rental income is generally not zakatable, though the rental income itself may be zakatable.

ZAKAT IN PAKISTAN
In Pakistan, Zakat is collected by the government under the Zakat and Ushr Ordinance 1980 for Sunni Muslims. Banks automatically deduct Zakat from savings accounts on the first of Ramadan each year if the balance exceeds the Nisab threshold. However, Muslims may declare exemption if they wish to pay Zakat directly to recipients of their choice. Shia Muslims are exempt from the government Zakat deduction.

COMMON QUESTIONS ABOUT ZAKAT

Is Zakat due on a salary received just before Ramadan?
If the salary brings your total wealth above Nisab at the end of your Hawl, then yes Zakat is due on the full amount. If you received the salary and spent most of it before your Hawl date, only what remains is zakatable.

Can I pay Zakat in advance?
Yes, a Muslim may pay Zakat before the Hawl is complete if they are confident their wealth will remain above Nisab. This is called Taajeel al-Zakat.

Can Zakat be paid to a family member?
Zakat cannot be paid to your parents, children, spouse, or anyone who you are financially responsible for. It can be paid to other relatives like siblings, cousins, uncles, and aunts if they are eligible.

Can Zakat be paid in installments?
Yes, Zakat can be paid monthly or in installments throughout the year, as long as the full calculated amount is paid by the end of the year.

Is Zakat due on a joint bank account?
Each co-owner of a joint account pays Zakat on their proportional share of the balance.
